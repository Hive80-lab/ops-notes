Ops notes

Practical notes on SOPs, checklists, AI workflows, and honest ops.

  • Browser Extension Security Checklist for Small Teams (The Attack Surface Hiding in Every Employee's Chrome)
    A quarterly 30-minute audit for the privileged code nobody counts: the inventory walk (ask the browser, not the person), the permission walk ("read all website data" is root), the zombie purge for abandoned extensions, update/ownership-change watch, and a four-sentence install policy — so the extension that went hostile stops being the easiest way into your webmail.
  • Remote Work Security Checklist for Small Teams (When the Office Is a Laptop in a Café)
    From home-router pre-flight to the BYOD decision: the admin lane that never shares a browser with the news site, hardware keys for money paths, guest-network zoning, and the coffee-shop rules that actually matter — with a written per-person pre-flight that makes the next audit take minutes.
  • GitHub Repository Security Checklist for Small Teams (Lock Down the Repo Before Someone Else Does)
    The 90-minute setup pass for the place your code lives: least-privilege member access with org 2FA enforced, branch protection that makes main annoying to break, secrets with push protection on and every committed token treated as burned, Dependabot triage that doubles as audit evidence, Actions hardened with pinned SHAs and a default-deny permissions block, the forgotten deploy keys and webhooks, offboarding rotations, and the monthly 30-minute pass that turns repo settings into customer-facing evidence.
  • Software Supply Chain Security Checklist for Small Teams (Your Code Is Fine — Your Dependencies Are Not)
    The half-day setup and one-hour quarterly pass for the incidents that start in somebody else's code: the dependency inventory that counts what you actually ship, subtraction as the highest-yield control (halve the count, one-question rule for new packages), lockfiles as load-bearing infrastructure, install-then-audit habits that replace the scanner you can't afford, CI actions pinned to SHAs with scoped tokens, digest-pinned base images, and the minute-by-minute response plan for the next big npm/PyPI/xz event — so the answer is a grep, not an archaeology dig.
  • Google Workspace Security Checklist for Small Business (Lock Down the Admin Console Before Someone Else Does)
    The ninety-minute setup pass and thirty-minute monthly ritual for the platform that holds everything: two named super admins plus a break-glass account, 2SV enforced with the exceptions audited, the OAuth app list turned from shadow IT into an approval queue, forwarding/delegation/filter rules checked against BEC, sharing defaults that match reality, suspend-first offboarding that keeps the mailbox, DKIM/SPF/DMARC, and the audit-trail export that turns the monthly pass into customer-facing evidence.
  • API Key Rotation Checklist for Small Teams (A 60-Minute Quarterly Ritual for the Keys Nobody Wants to Touch)
    The quarterly ritual that ends forever-keys: an inventory sweep that finds the keys nobody remembers making (provider consoles are ground truth), the rotate/shrink/retire decision per key, the no-downtime sequence — create → deploy → verify → revoke, with one watched cron cycle between — the CI/CD and deploy-platform secrets that quietly hold half your keys, shared-key kills, vendor keys you don't control, and the rotation log that turns the scariest maintenance task into routine hygiene.
  • Website Maintenance Checklist for Small Businesses (The 30-Minute Monthly Routine)
    The four quiet failures that kill small-business websites (expired certificate, expired domain, silently broken contact form, backup that never restores), a 30-minute monthly pass with minute budgets, the quarterly restore test, and update habits that don't turn Friday 5pm into an outage.
  • IoT Device Security Checklist for Small Offices (Printers, Cameras, and the Forgotten Attack Surface)
    A 90-minute + quarterly ritual for the device fleet nobody owns: the 60-minute inventory walk, default credentials killed in one session, firmware as a calendar event, a segregated device VLAN that beats patching, the internet-exposure audit (UPnP and abandoned port-forwards), printer scan-to-email credentials, and camera/NVR hygiene — so a breach that starts on a $300 device stops being the easy way in.
  • Customer Security Questionnaire Response Template for Small Teams (Answer Enterprise Buyers in One Afternoon)
    The one-afternoon system for the questionnaire that stalls the deal: an evidence folder that backs every answer (controls.md, dated screenshots, tested-backup logs, the subprocessor list), the twelve repeatable answers enterprise buyers actually score, the three-part honest answer for what you don't have yet, the red flags inside the buyer's own form (mandatory SSO, proportionality, subprocessor-deep legal review), and the reuse rule that makes questionnaire #2 cost ninety minutes — so the security review becomes a deal accelerator, not a two-week stall.
  • Customer Data Deletion Request Checklist for Small Teams (The 30-Day Clock After “Delete My Data”)
    The two-hour playbook for the email that freezes small teams: acknowledge within 48 hours and verify the requester is the customer (fake DSARs are an account-takeover play), a free-text-search inventory that finds the copies, the delete-vs-keep split tax law forces, the processor relay to every SaaS that holds the data, what "deleted" means when backups exist, and the closing letter that ends the clock — so one request becomes evidence of compliance, not a compliance incident.
  • Domain Expiry & Hijack Protection Checklist for Small Business (The One Asset You Can’t Re-Issue)
    The one hour of setup and ten-minute quarterly drill that keep the name itself safe: multi-year registration with billing alarms at 60/30/7 days, transfer lock and the EPP-code rules, nameserver-change emails treated as incidents, the circular recovery-email trap, ownership under the business entity, a one-page domain record, and the first-hour sequence when a transfer happens anyway.
  • Invoice Fraud & Business Email Compromise Prevention Checklist for Small Business (Before the Wire Clears)
    The payables playbook against the email that looks like accounting work: bank changes verified by callback on the ledger number, a two-person rule that has no urgent bypass, the four fraud plays and their tells, mailbox hardening, the first-hour wire-recall sequence, and a quarterly drill — so paying a fraudster is never the normal path.
  • Annual Security Review Checklist for Small Business (One Morning, Before the Insurance Renewal)
    The half-day review that answers the questions the insurance renewal and the next incident will ask: a live restore test, secrets rotated, access audited against actual people, one internal phish, machinery checked, and a one-page record of tested / fixed / accepted — so the answers exist before anyone asks under deadline.
  • Passkeys for Small Teams: A Rollout That Doesn’t Lock Anyone Out
    The rollout that keeps a second live path to every account: break-glass recovery before the first passkey, a per-vendor support inventory, platform 2FA fixed first, the password removed last (per person, with a date), the shared-login decision written down, and the offboarding hook that revokes passkeys — not just passwords.
  • Vendor Offboarding & Data Deletion Checklist for Small Teams (Exit Without the Exposure)
    The exit that actually closes: read the exit clause before cancelling, export what you own while admin access lasts, untangle SSO apps and API keys, revoke every door, request deletion in writing with a date, and verify instead of trusting — so a fired vendor doesn't stay a silent liability on your next insurance application.
  • Email Deliverability Checklist for Small Teams (SPF, DKIM & DMARC Without Breaking Mail)
    The 45-minute audit that keeps invoices, resets and MFA codes out of spam: one SPF record inside the 10-lookup budget, 2048-bit DKIM on two live selectors, DMARC walked from p=none to reject on a report-driven schedule, and the Monday-morning runbook for when mail stops arriving.

  • SaaS Sprawl Audit for Small Teams (Find the Tools You Forgot You're Paying For)
    The one-hour audit that finds every subscription nobody owns: the 90-day statement pull, one owner per tool, kill/keep/consolidate, seat cleanup, and the renewal calendar that stops the creep.
  • Password Manager Rollout for Small Teams (the Order That Avoids the 2AM Lockout)
    A rollout where every step leaves you safer even if the project dies there: break-glass recovery tested before a single password moves, inventory before import, the worst passwords rotated during the pilot, shared logins into shared collections (never personal vaults), a real decommission date for the spreadsheet, and the offboarding hook most teams wire too late.
  • Vendor Security Review Checklist for Small Teams (12 Questions Before You Sign)
    The one-afternoon review that keeps a vendor’s breach from becoming yours: the 12 questions to ask before signing (data inventory, breach history, MFA/SSO, sub-processors, a notification term in hours, exit deletion), the red-flag answers that slow a deal, and the tiering that keeps the whole thing to a spreadsheet.
  • Lost or Stolen Laptop: The First Hour (Revoke, Wipe, Document)
    Two incidents at once — physical loss and access loss. The first-ten-minutes order (identity reset + sign-out everywhere + MFA device revocation + remote wipe + network revocation), what the disk can actually hold and how to rotate it, the paperwork sequence, and the encryption-by-default policy that turns the next theft into an insurance claim instead of a breach.
  • Cyber Insurance for Small Teams (The 12 Controls the Application Actually Checks)
    The application is the underwriting: the twelve controls small-business forms actually verify — MFA, tested (not just existing) backups, EDR, patching records, offboarding, a one-page IR plan — plus the answers that quietly void a claim and the evidence folder to build before you apply.
  • API Key Leaked: the First-Hour Runbook for Small Teams
    The safe order that avoids the self-inflicted outage: scope the blast radius first, create the replacement key before you revoke, check usage for real abuse, purge the quiet copies (CI logs, tickets, dotfiles), and the 30-minute prevention pass that stops the next leak.
  • MFA Rollout Checklist for Small Teams (Enforce It Without Locking Anyone Out)
    A rollout that does not flood the helpdesk: inventory before settings, break-glass admins and per-person recovery codes before enforcement, a one-week volunteer pilot that surfaces the broken integrations, the enforcement-day sequence, and the mistakes (SMS-only, optional-forever, one phone per shared account) that make rollouts fail.
  • Phishing Response Checklist for Small Teams (The First Hour After Someone Clicks)
    The one-hour playbook for the morning someone clicks: contain in ten minutes (sessions, mailbox rules, OAuth grants), scope what the attacker actually got, revoke sessions before resetting passwords, and the report-the-click culture that keeps the next click fast.
  • Log Retention Policy for Small Teams (What to Keep, How Long, and the One-Command Check)
    The five numbers that answer the auditor's question: tiered retention per log type (auth 90d/12mo, access 30–90d, cloud audit, app, security events), logs that land off-box and append-only, a short never-log list, and lifecycle rules plus a weekly oldest-object check that enforce deletion without anyone remembering.
  • Suspected Data Breach: the First 24 Hours for Small Teams
    Two clocks run at once: confirm scope before you announce, contain without destroying evidence (export logs before rotating, isolate instead of wipe), hit the notification clocks with counsel early, and tell customers the true shape of the data — categories, risk, and the exact steps they should take.
  • AI Agent Permission Boundaries Checklist (What Your Agent May Touch)
    An AI agent is a junior teammate with infinite enthusiasm: read-only by default, writes behind human gates that an agent earns by boring weeks, one scoped rotatable identity that can't read its own credentials, ceilings from the outside, an append-only action log, and a kill switch that doesn't need consent.
  • Runaway Automation Runbook for Small Teams (Kill Switches, Ceilings, Idempotency)
    When automation misbehaves it keeps going: one-command kill switches for cron, queues, agents, and integrations; ceilings that live outside the automation (max runs, max spend, dead-letters, breakers); idempotency so re-runs can't double-charge; and the five-line post-incident checklist.
  • Disk Full Incident Runbook for Small Teams (Safe Cleanup Order, Usual Suspects, the 80% Line)
    The incident that takes friends down with it: buy breathing room without deleting evidence (truncate, never rm what you can't explain), the boring usual suspects (logs, journals, Docker layers, colliding backups, core dumps), the safe cleanup order, and the guardrails that stop the sequel.
  • Cron Job Monitoring Checklist for Small Teams (Silent Failures, Dead Jobs, Heartbeats)
    Cron fails twice and teams only notice once: heartbeat every critical job to catch the schedules that silently stop, alert on runtime and output (hangs, zeros, drift) not just exit codes, keep an owned inventory of every schedule, and the 15-minute setup with a tested silence alert.
  • Vendor Outage Runbook for Small Teams (When the Outage Isn't Yours)
    The outage where your code is fine and your status page still goes silent: classify yours-vs-theirs in 10 minutes, degrade deliberately (wait / feature-flag / failover), communicate on a clock with the vendor named, and harvest the dependency list that shortens every future outage.
  • Cloud Cost Spike Runbook for Small Teams (Top Movers, the Usual Suspects, Free Guardrails)
    Nobody catches a bill spike by reading the invoice: triage by dollar change and tag, drill to the start date, fix the boring causes (egress, NAT, log retention, retry storms, forgotten dev monsters), then add the free guardrail — forecast budget alert, anomaly alert, tag discipline, auto-stop for ephemeral environments.
  • DNS Outage Runbook for Small Teams (TTLs, the Hidden Second Nameserver, and the 10-Minute Restore)
    The runbook for the outage that never appears in your logs: watch the domain itself (expiry, NS set, cross-resolver answers), keep a tested zone copy at a second provider, lower TTLs before changes instead of during fires, and the exact 10-minute restore path when DNS is already down.
  • SSL Certificate Expiry Checklist for Small Teams (Expiry Alerts, Renewal Dry-Runs, and the Already-Expired Runbook)
    The four-part fix for the Friday-night cert outage: inventory every cert (mail, VPN, panels, not just the site), alert on the certificate actually served rather than the one configured, rehearse the renewal quarterly because "it's automated" is a hypothesis, and keep the runbook for when it has already expired.
  • Secrets Rotation Checklist for Small Teams (Rotate What Leaks, Not Everything)
