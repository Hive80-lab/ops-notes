Ops notes

Practical notes on SOPs, checklists, AI workflows, and honest ops.

  • Ops Starter Kit — Incident Response for Small Teams ($14)
    The paid kit the free notes plug into: a fillable incident response plan, a 3-level severity matrix, tabletop scenarios, and send-ready comms templates. Fill the blanks once in about 20 minutes — so when the pager fires at 2AM, the plan is already filled in.
  • AI-Powered Incident Response System
    Complete AI-powered incident response: frameworks, runbooks, playbooks for autonomous outage management. Severity matrix, AI-assisted triage, automated response playbooks, post-incident learning loops.
  • API Key Leaked: the First-Hour Runbook for Small Teams
    The safe order that avoids the self-inflicted outage: scope the blast radius first, create the replacement key before you revoke, check usage for real abuse, purge the quiet copies (CI logs, tickets, dotfiles), and the 30-minute prevention pass that stops the next leak.
  • MFA Rollout Checklist for Small Teams (Enforce It Without Locking Anyone Out)
    A rollout that does not flood the helpdesk: inventory before settings, break-glass admins and per-person recovery codes before enforcement, a one-week volunteer pilot that surfaces the broken integrations, the enforcement-day sequence, and the mistakes (SMS-only, optional-forever, one phone per shared account) that make rollouts fail.
  • Phishing Response Checklist for Small Teams (The First Hour After Someone Clicks)
    The one-hour playbook for the morning someone clicks: contain in ten minutes (sessions, mailbox rules, OAuth grants), scope what the attacker actually got, revoke sessions before resetting passwords, and the report-the-click culture that keeps the next click fast.
  • Log Retention Policy for Small Teams (What to Keep, How Long, and the One-Command Check)
    The five numbers that answer the auditor's question: tiered retention per log type (auth 90d/12mo, access 30-90d, cloud audit, app, security events), logs that land off-box and append-only, a short never-log list, and lifecycle rules plus a weekly oldest-object check that enforce deletion without anyone remembering.
  • Suspected Data Breach: the First 24 Hours for Small Teams
    Two clocks run at once: confirm scope before you announce, contain without destroying evidence (export logs before rotating, isolate instead of wipe), hit the notification clocks with counsel early, and tell customers the true shape of the data — categories, risk, and the exact steps they should take.
  • AI Agent Permission Boundaries Checklist (What Your Agent May Touch)
    An AI agent is a junior teammate with infinite enthusiasm: read-only by default, writes behind human gates that an agent earns by boring weeks, one scoped rotatable identity that can't read its own credentials, ceilings from the outside, an append-only action log, and a kill switch that doesn't need consent.
  • Runaway Automation Runbook for Small Teams (Kill Switches, Ceilings, Idempotency)
    When automation misbehaves it keeps going: one-command kill switches for cron, queues, agents, and integrations; ceilings that live outside the automation (max runs, max spend, dead-letters, breakers); idempotency so re-runs can't double-charge; and the five-line post-incident checklist.
  • Disk Full Incident Runbook for Small Teams (Safe Cleanup Order, Usual Suspects, the 80% Line)
    The incident that takes friends down with it: buy breathing room without deleting evidence (truncate, never rm what you can't explain), the boring usual suspects (logs, build cache, databases, conda/pip envs, user data), and the 80% rule that catches the expensive cleanup before you delete.
  • Server Hardening Checklist for Small Teams
    SSH, SSL, firewall, log policies, secrets rotation, two-factor authentication, and asset inventory — the non-negotiables for secure remote access and infrastructure.
