What This Is

A supply chain is just a collection of vendors that each deliver one piece of your business. If any piece fails, you're broken. This playbook gives you a systematic way to identify where you're vulnerable, build redundancy, and run a 30-minute disaster drill every quarter to keep your plans fresh.

Why You Need This

  • Collapse risk: Single supplier failure can shut your operations within hours. Don't assume it can't happen to you.
  • Fatigue: When a disruption hits, you're stressed and prone to bad decisions. Planning removes the panic.
  • Insurance evidence: Most contracts require documented continuity plans. This is your proof.
  • Customer confidence: Clients trust you when they know you have backups.

Step 2: Identify Single Points of Failure (30 Minutes)

After you map your supply chain, identify which vendors have no backup. These are your single points of failure.

Failure Impact Calculator

For each critical/high-tier vendor, calculate the impact if they fail:

Vendor Failure Probability (1-5) Impact Level (1-5) Risk Score (Prob × Impact)
[Vendor Name] [1-5] [1-5] [Score]
[Vendor Name] [1-5] [1-5] [Score]
Risk Score Interpretation:
  • Score 5-10: Moderate risk. Focus on improving availability/backup.
  • Score 11-25: High risk. Get backup immediately or accept the risk.
  • Score 26+: Critical risk. Do something or rename your business "High-Risk."

Step 3: Build Backup Vendors (Ongoing)

For every critical/high-risk vendor, identify a backup vendor before you need them. This usually means qualifying 2-3 vendors and signing a small trial contract.

Backup Qualification Checklist

  • Capacity check: Can they handle your volume?
  • Capability check: Can they deliver what you need?
  • Cost check: Do you have budget for them?
  • Credential check: Do they have the necessary certifications?
  • Availability check: Can they start immediately?
  • Documentation check: Can they provide the same reports and SLA?
Example Backup Implementation:

Your hosting provider fails. You have an emergency backup vendor. They can spin up your servers in 2 hours because you qualified them 3 months ago and already have a small "rescue" account. Your business doesn't miss a beat.

Step 4: Create Recovery Playbooks (2 Hours)

For every critical supplier, write a 1-page playbook that tells you exactly what to do when they fail. Update it every quarter.

Recovery Playbook Template

Supplier [Vendor Name]
Failure Symptom [e.g., Website down, no access to servers]
Activation Checklist
  1. Confirm failure with customer support
  2. Document timeline and severity
  3. Notify stakeholders (internal + external)
  4. Initiate backup vendor contract
  5. Assign POCs to both primary and backup
  6. Set up daily status meetings
Communication Template To: Stakeholders
From: Supply Chain Team
Subject: [Vendor Name] Failure - Active Response

We are actively managing a failure with [Vendor Name]. Here's what we know:

  • When: [Timestamp]
  • What: [Failure description]
  • Impact: [Customer impact]
  • Response: [Action items and timeline]

We will provide updates every [timeframe]. Questions? Contact [Name/Title].
Success Criteria [e.g., Systems operational within 4 hours, customer impact below 5%, no data loss]
Post-Incident Report Complete within 3 days: timeline, root cause, what we learned, changes to prevent recurrence.

Step 5: Run Monthly Disaster Drills (30 Minutes)

A plan that's never tested is a plan that won't work. Pick one critical supplier and simulate their failure. See how long it takes you to activate your backup.

Drill Agenda (30 Minutes)
  1. Selection (5 min): Pick the most vulnerable supplier this month.
  2. Simulation (10 min): Pretend they've failed. Call their support line. Document response time.
  3. Backup Activation (10 min): Activate your backup vendor. How long? What's broken? What needs fixing?
  4. Review (5 min): What did we learn? Update the playbook if needed.
Drill Example:

You simulate your primary payment processor failing. You activate the backup processor in 47 minutes. The backup is 23% slower for integrations. You identify that you need to refactor your payment flow or qualify another processor. Problem solved before the real failure happens.

Step 6: Inventory and Buffer Stock (For Physical Goods)

If your business relies on physical goods, you need safety stock and buffer inventory. The rule of thumb: keep 2-3 weeks of critical supplies on hand.

Buffer Stock Guidelines

  • Critical items: 4-6 weeks of supply on hand (minimum 2)
  • High-impact items: 2-3 weeks of supply on hand
  • Moderate items: 1 week of supply on hand
  • Low-impact items: No buffer stock (replenish after each order)

Step 7: Financial Contingency Planning

If a supply chain disruption costs you 30 days, do you have the cash flow to survive it? Map your break-even point and keep a contingency fund.

Break-Even Analysis

Fixed Costs / Day [Calculate: rent, salaries, utilities, software, etc.]
Gross Revenue / Day [Your actual number]
Break-Even Point [Fixed Costs / Gross Revenue]
Contingency Days [Break-Even × 2]
Example:

You have $10,000 in fixed costs daily and generate $15,000 in revenue daily. Your break-even point is 66%. If a disruption costs you 30 days, you need $300,000 in cash. Set aside $350,000 in your contingency fund.

Worked Example

The $4.2M Warning Shot

Your primary warehouse provider commits to 99% uptime. You have no backup. Six months later, a storm knocks out their power for 48 hours. Your inventory is stuck. Revenue drops 82%. You lose $4.2M in sales that quarter.

The fix: You qualified 3 backup warehouse providers. Within 4 hours of the outage, you moved your entire inventory to Backup Provider A. You signed a 6-month contract with Backup Provider A and Backup Provider B (keeping them on retainer). You still lost 4 days of sales, but you reduced the revenue impact by 76%.

Lesson: You never know when a disruption will hit. Plan for it anyway.

Key Metrics to Track

  • Single Point of Failure (SPoF) Count: Number of critical suppliers with no backup. Target: 0
  • Drill Pass Rate: Percentage of drills where you hit your target recovery time. Target: 100%
  • Recovery Time (MTTR): Mean Time to Recover from a supplier failure. Target: Under 4 hours for critical items
  • Stockout Rate: Frequency of running out of critical inventory. Target: 0% for critical items
  • Contingency Fund Utilization: How often you use your contingency fund. Use this to adjust sizing.
