Incident Response Checklist for Small Teams

Updated 2026 - 8 min read - Free template included

#DevOps#IncidentResponse#Operations#SmallBusiness

When production goes down at 3am, you don't need theory. You need a checklist that tells you exactly what to do, in what order, and who to call.

This checklist has been refined over hundreds of real incidents. It works for teams of 1-10 people who run production without a dedicated SRE team.

Phase 1: First 30 Minutes

  • Confirm the incident is real. Check monitoring dashboards, not just alerts.
  • Say it out loud: "I think we have an incident." This shifts your brain from panic to process.
  • Assign severity: SEV1 (business stopped), SEV2 (degraded), SEV3 (minor)
  • Page the right people. Not everyone. Just who can fix it and who needs to know.
  • Freeze all deployments. No new code goes out until resolved.
  • Announce the next update time. "Next update in 15 minutes." Then actually update.

Phase 2: Investigation (30 min - 2 hours)

  • Create a dedicated incident channel. Slack/Teams. Invite only relevant people.
  • Post the initial message: What's broken, what we know, what we're doing, next update time.
  • Assign roles: Incident Commander, Scribe, Investigator.
  • Check the timeline: What changed in the last 24 hours?
  • Check dependencies: Is a third-party service down?
  • Update every 15 minutes. Even if "still investigating."

Phase 3: Recovery

  • Identify root cause - not just the symptom.
  • Test the fix in staging first. Always. Even for config changes.
  • Deploy the fix. Watch the deployment closely.
  • Verify recovery. Monitoring should be green, not just "no errors."
  • Monitor for 30 minutes before declaring resolved.
  • Announce resolution. Post in the incident channel and to stakeholders.

Phase 4: Post-Incident

  • Schedule a blameless post-mortem within 48 hours.
  • Write the timeline of events.
  • Identify contributing factors. Not just root cause.
  • Create action items with owners and deadlines.
  • Share learnings with the wider team.
Common mistake: Skipping the post-mortem because "it was a small incident." Small incidents are patterns. Patterns become outages.
