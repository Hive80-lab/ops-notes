Business Continuity Plan Template for Small Business

Free template + step-by-step guide. A business continuity plan (BCP) answers one question: when the worst happens, how does the business keep running? Here's the template small teams use — no consultants, no 200-page documents, just the decisions that matter.

What a Business Continuity Plan Actually Does

A BCP is the document your team opens when something fundamental has gone wrong — not just a server crash, but a situation that disrupts how the business operates. Office inaccessible. Key person unavailable. Vendor goes dark. Legal crisis. The BCP tells you what to do in the first hour, the first day, and the first week.

The mistake most small businesses make: treating the BCP as a compliance document. It's not. It's an operational tool. If your team can't pick it up and act on it in 10 minutes, it's too long.

The Four Disruptions Your BCP Must Cover

Scenario 1: Workforce Disruption

Office inaccessible, key person unavailable, mass illness, evacuation.

  • ☐ Where does the team work if the office is closed? (remote, secondary site, home)
  • ☐ Who has access to critical systems remotely? (list names + systems)
  • ☐ How does the team communicate? (group chat, phone tree, email alias)
  • ☐ What's the minimum team size to keep operating? (who's essential?)
  • ☐ Cross-training: who can cover for the missing person? (documented, not assumed)
  • ☐ Payroll continuity: how does payroll run if finance is unavailable?

Scenario 2: System Failure

Core system down, data loss, cyberattack, cloud provider outage.

  • ☐ Which systems are critical vs. nice-to-have? (ranked list)
  • ☐ For each critical system: backup location, recovery steps, RTO/RPO
  • ☐ Who declares a system failure vs. a disaster? (escalation threshold)
  • ☐ Cyberattack response: who isolates, who investigates, who communicates?
  • ☐ Customer notification template (pre-written, fill-in-the-blanks)

Link to your DR plan: The BCP references your disaster recovery plan for IT-specific recovery steps. Don't duplicate — point.

Scenario 3: Vendor/Supply Chain Disruption

Critical vendor goes out of business, supply chain breaks, SaaS provider has extended outage.

  • ☐ List of critical vendors (ranked by business impact if they fail)
  • ☐ For each: alternative vendor identified? (yes/no + name)
  • ☐ Contract exit terms documented? (notice period, data export, transition support)
  • ☐ Data portability: can you export your data from this vendor? (format, frequency)
  • ☐ Vendor communication plan: who contacts the vendor, who tracks the response?

Scenario 4: Reputation/Legal Crisis

Data breach, customer lawsuit, regulatory inquiry, public relations crisis.

  • ☐ Who is the spokesperson? (one person, named in advance)
  • ☐ Legal counsel contact (name, phone, retainer status)
  • ☐ Insurance contact (policy number, coverage type, claim process)
  • ☐ Customer communication: pre-approved templates for breach notification
  • ☐ Internal communication: what to tell employees, what NOT to tell employees
  • ☐ Regulatory notification: which agencies, what deadlines, who files?

The One-Page BCP Template

Print this. Fill it in. Tape it to the wall.

Business Continuity Plan — [Company Name]

Last updated: [date] | Next review: [date + 90 days]

Plan owner: [name, phone, email]

Alternate owner: [name, phone, email]

Critical Systems (ranked)

  1. [System 1] — Owner: [name] — RTO: [hours] — Backup: [location]
  2. [System 2] — Owner: [name] — RTO: [hours] — Backup: [location]
  3. [System 3] — Owner: [name] — RTO: [hours] — Backup: [location]

Critical Vendors (ranked)

  1. [Vendor 1] — Service: [what they do] — Alt: [backup vendor] — Exit: [notice period]
  2. [Vendor 2] — Service: [what they do] — Alt: [backup vendor] — Exit: [notice period]

Emergency Contacts

  • Team lead: [name, phone]
  • IT/Systems: [name, phone]
  • Legal: [name, phone]
  • Insurance: [provider, policy #, phone]
  • Key vendor contacts: [list]

Communication Templates

  • Internal alert: "We are activating the BCP due to [event]. Team leads, check in at [time]. Next update at [time]."
  • Customer notice: "We are experiencing [issue]. We're working on it. We'll update you by [time]."
  • All-clear: "The situation is resolved. [Service] is operating normally. We'll share a summary within [n] days."

How to Test Your BCP (Without a Real Crisis)

A BCP that's never been tested is a theory. Here's the 30-minute test every small team should run quarterly:

  1. ☐ Pick one scenario (e.g., "office is closed for 3 days")
  2. ☐ Set a timer for 10 minutes
  3. ☐ Have the plan owner open the BCP and walk through what they'd do
  4. ☐ Time how long it takes to find every contact, every backup location, every recovery step
  5. ☐ Note anything that's missing, outdated, or unclear
  6. ☐ Fix one thing before the next test

If the plan owner can't get through the scenario in 10 minutes, the plan is too complex. Simplify it.

The Most Common BCP Failure

Stale contacts. The #1 reason BCPs fail in real crises: the phone numbers don't work anymore. The vendor contact left. The IT person moved on. The insurance broker changed. A BCP with outdated contacts is worse than no plan — it creates false confidence.

Review contacts every 90 days. Set a calendar reminder. Send a test message to every contact on the list. If it bounces, fix it that day.

Common Questions

Do I need a BCP if I'm a solo founder?

Yes — especially if you're solo. If you're the only person who knows how everything works, a BCP ensures someone else can step in if you can't. Document your systems, passwords (in a password manager, not the BCP), and recovery steps so a contractor or family member can keep the business running.

How long should a small business BCP be?

One to five pages. If it's longer, you're writing for compliance, not for use. The test: can someone pick it up at 2am and act on it? If they need to read 20 pages first, it's too long.

What's the difference between a BCP and an incident response plan?

An incident response plan covers a specific type of event (security breach, system outage). A BCP covers the business-wide impact: how does the business keep running while the incident is being resolved? Your IR plan feeds into your BCP.
