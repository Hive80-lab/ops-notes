How the calculator works

The calculator uses a simple model that captures the four costs of an incident that most businesses underestimate:

  • Lost revenue: (Annual revenue / 2000 working hours) × outage hours × revenue dependency %. Most businesses run 2000 working hours per year; if 80% of your revenue depends on the affected system, a 4-hour outage costs 4 × (revenue/2000) × 0.80.
  • Staff time: Employees affected × hourly rate × outage hours. This is the productive time lost while staff wait, work around, or firefight. It doesn't include the recovery overtime.
  • Customer churn: Customers affected × churn rate × average customer annual value. Not every affected customer leaves, but some do. A 5% churn rate on 50 customers worth $2,000/year is $5,000 in lost future revenue.
  • Recovery & remediation: Estimated at 50% of the lost revenue + staff cost. This covers the post-incident fix, the postmortem, the customer follow-up, and the "make sure this never happens again" work. For small teams, this is usually 1-3 days of focused effort.

Why most businesses underestimate incident costs

The number on the calculator is usually 3-5x higher than what businesses expect. Here's why:

  • They count only the IT cost (staff time) and forget the revenue cost. A 4-hour outage for a $2M business with 80% revenue dependency costs $3,200 in lost revenue alone — before anyone picks up a phone.
  • They don't count customer churn. The customer who leaves after an outage doesn't show up on the incident report. They show up three months later in the revenue numbers, and nobody connects the two.
  • They don't count recovery time. The outage ends at hour 4, but the postmortem, the fix, the customer emails, and the "let's make sure this doesn't happen again" meeting take another 1-2 days. That's staff time too.
  • They don't count the second incident. Teams without a postmortem process repeat the same incident. The second outage costs the same as the first, plus the credibility damage.
