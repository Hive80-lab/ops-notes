A two-star review lands at 7:12am: *"waited 20 minutes, cold coffee, staff acted like I was the problem."* The instinct — defend, explain, argue, fast, in public — is exactly wrong. The reply was never written to the reviewer; it's written to the next fifty people deciding where to spend their money.

The playbook in this note is one page, written in peacetime:

- **The 24-hour rule, and who holds the pen** — the owner or manager replies, never the named employee, and only once.
- **The fact-check before a single word is typed** — find the visit in the till and the camera, hear the story without demanding a confession, then decide which of three it is: we failed / it felt that way / it didn't happen as described.
- **The five-part reply anatomy** — thank and acknowledge the *specific* thing; own the fixable part; the offline move with one named person; the new-state line ("we've moved a second barista to Saturdays"); stop.
- **What never goes in a public reply** — the word "fake," the receipt-by-receipt defense, the identical paste, the 11pm owner rant.
- **The make-good ladder** — fix it forward, credit, refund — with one rule above it: *the call comes before the credit*.
- **Fake reviews: flag, don't fight.** Post the dignified "we can't find a record of this visit" line anyway; spend the energy on the fresh-review bench, not the dispute queue.
- **The theme log** — once is an incident, twice is a pattern, three mentions is a policy decision at the Monday huddle.
- **The fresh-review bench** — ask at the moment of delight, name the platform, two taps from the counter QR. One two-star among four reads as a bad business; the same two-star among forty reads as a bad Tuesday.

The worked example: a twelve-seat café, a buried bar ticket, a cold flat white. Fact-check by 7:20, public reply inside the 24-hour window (four sentences, zero defensiveness), the owner's two-minute call by 11:30, the process fix at the Monday huddle — and two weeks later the reviewer's own four-star update outranks her complaint. Net: zero dollars of discount, one process fix, one repeat customer who now recommends the café *because of* how its worst morning was handled.
