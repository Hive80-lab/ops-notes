Quarterly Business Review Template: The 90-Minute Cadence That Turns Data Into Decisions

The quick version: most quarterly reviews are data-dumps that end with "good discussion" and no actions. The problem isn't the data—it's that reviews treat every metric equally and skip the three questions that actually matter: what changed, why it changed, and what we're doing about it. This page gives a 90-minute quarterly review template that produces one page of decisions, not a folder of charts: 30 minutes of focused prep, 45 minutes of structured review, and 15 minutes of action commitment, with the leading indicators that predict trouble before it arrives.

Why quarterly reviews become data-dumps

Quarterly reviews fail when they try to cover everything. The revenue chart goes up, the customer count goes up, the expenses go up—three hours later, everyone leaves with a clearer view of the past and no plan for the future. The failure isn't the presentation; it's that the review treated all metrics as equally important and never asked the three questions that drive action: what actually changed this quarter, why did it change, and what are we doing about it?

A data-dump review presents information. A decision review extracts the three things that changed the trajectory and commits to actions that change the next trajectory. The first feels productive; the second is productive.

The 90-minute structure

Part 1: 30 minutes pre-meeting prep (individual)

Each leader answers three questions about their area, in one document:

  1. What changed? The one metric or trend that shifted direction this quarter. Not everything—pick the one that matters.
  2. Why did it change? The specific action, market shift, or decision that caused it. No speculation—stick to what you can prove.
  3. What's the plan? One specific action for next quarter, with an owner and a metric that shows if it worked.

Part 2: 45 minutes structured review (group)

TimeActivityOutput
0-10 minRound robin: each person's "what changed" (2 minutes max)3-5 key changes identified
10-25 minDeep dive: pick the 2 changes that affect the whole businessRoot causes confirmed
25-35 minLeading indicators: what metrics would have warned us earlier?2-3 predictive metrics added
35-45 minAction commit: each change gets one owner, one deadline, one success metricAction items locked

Part 3: 15 minutes summary capture

One person fills the one-page summary while the group watches:

  • The three changes that mattered (what, why, impact)
  • The three actions we're taking (owner, deadline, success metric)
  • The three leading indicators we're watching (metric, threshold, who tracks)

Leading vs lagging indicators that matter

Lagging indicators tell you what happened:

  • Revenue this quarter
  • Customer count
  • Profit margin

Leading indicators tell you what will happen:

  • Sales pipeline velocity (deals per week)
  • Customer onboarding completion rate
  • Feature adoption rate for new releases
  • Team capacity utilization (hours booked vs available)

The mistake is reviewing only lagging indicators. The insight is tracking the leading indicators that would have predicted the lagging ones.

The follow-up rhythm that sticks

Quarterly reviews die when the actions disappear until the next quarterly review. The fix is a simple follow-up rhythm:

  • Week 1 after review: Each action owner posts a 1-sentence update in Slack
  • Month 1 after review: 15-minute check-in on the leading indicators
  • Month 2 after review: Review if the actions are on track or need adjustment
  • Month 3 (next quarter): Results become part of the next review

Common quarterly review traps

  • The everything review: Trying to cover every metric instead of the three that changed the trajectory.
  • The speculation session: Discussing why something might have happened without data to prove it.
  • The action list without owners: "We should improve X" without a person who owns the improvement.
  • The leading indicator blind spot: Only reviewing lagging metrics and wondering why you're always surprised.
  • The follow-up vacuum: Creating a great plan that disappears until the next quarterly meeting.

Implementation checklist

  • [ ] Send pre-meeting questions 3 days before the review
  • [ ] Collect individual responses 1 day before
  • [ ] Identify the 2-3 changes that affect the whole business
  • [ ] Pick 2-3 leading indicators to track going forward
  • [ ] Assign one owner to each action with a specific deadline
  • [ ] Schedule the month 1 check-in before leaving the room
  • [ ] Post the one-page summary within 24 hours
