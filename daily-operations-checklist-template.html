Daily Operations Checklist Template (The 3-Zone Format)

Most daily checklists fail for a boring reason: they're a pile of tasks with no

structure, so on a bad day everything looks equally urgent. The 3-zone format fixes

that by ordering the day by consequence, not by convenience: money first, people

second, systems third.

Zone 1 — Money (do first, every day)

Anything with a deadline, a payment, or a number someone else is waiting on:

  • [ ] Invoices to send / overdue invoices to chase
  • [ ] Payments or transfers scheduled or due today
  • [ ] Cash-flow number you actually look at (one number, same place, same time)

Why first: money failures compound silently. A missed payment becomes a fee becomes a

relationship problem; a chased invoice today is cash this week.

Zone 2 — People (promises and blockers)

  • [ ] Promises made yesterday that come due today
  • [ ] Anything blocked waiting on you
  • [ ] One conversation you've been avoiding (15 minutes, today)

Why second: people zone failures are trust withdrawals. They're invisible until they

aren't, and the check is cheap.

Zone 3 — Systems (health, not polish)

  • [ ] Backups verified (not just "running") — a restore test weekly beats a green

light daily

  • [ ] Alerts or logs scanned for anything new since yesterday
  • [ ] One maintenance item moved forward, however small

Why third: systems work is important but rarely urgent today — which is exactly why

it gets a fixed, protected slot instead of competing with money and people.

Rules that make it stick

  1. Same time daily. A checklist without a time is a suggestion.
  2. Max 12 boxes. More than that and the day manages the checklist, not the reverse.
  3. One owner per zone. Shared checklists need named checkboxes or they rot.
  4. Miss is data, not guilt. Skip a zone two days running? Change the checklist —

it's telling you something.
